Late spring was a good time for running, with the air being warm, but not too hot, and the sun out for longer. The sight of budding trees and flowers made it much nicer as well. This was Raffina's favorite time for her daily runs through town. The perfect weather invigorated her the best.

It helped that she now had a running partner, who was just as invigorating. "Heeey Raffina! Wait up! I'm still behind"! Amitie yells from afar, in between panting. She ran a distance behind Raffina.

"Ohoho! No can do, Ami! You're going to have to work harder to catch up!" Raffina joyfully yells back. She smiled at her own accomplishment, but that wasn't all. Looking back again at Amitie and how hard she was trying only made her want to try harder herself. She was determined and it rubbed off on her, much like the sunkight warming her skin.

Raffina would never admit it, but spending time around this girl had made her happier. She had this infectious energy to her, and lately Raffina had given in and let herself take some of that joy for herself. Amitie really felt brighter than the sun sometimes. They weren't just rivals anymore, but maybe something more like friends. Just maybe.

As Raffina thought on it, she finally noticed Amitie catching up, just as they reached their running milestone. It was that energy combined with her determined attitude that let her catch up, and Raffina felt proud of her for doing it. They both had huge smiles on their faces as they finished and went about their separate ways. Raffina lingered a little longer though, just looking at Amitie.

She felt nice. Not just from the buzz of a good workout, but from being around her too. She was on her mind and in her sights more often, and it just felt nice. There was no other way she could really put it. Well, maybe… it was more than nice. But there was no reason it had to be anything more than that, and certainly no need to reveal such a feeling to anyone.

She coughed suddenly, and it snapped her out of her thoughts again. When she looked at her arm, she saw a single flower petal. It perplexed her for a second, but she didn't think much of it, and continued her business.

Only a few days ago, Raffina began experiencing symptoms that seemed like a cold. Her chest and throat felt tight, her nose was stuffed, and she coughed all the time. It was already upsetting enough; Raffina never got sick, she was always in top shape! Yet somehow it had happened anyway, and nothing seemed to make it much better. The worst part was that breathing was harder, and the exhaustion made it more difficult to get her workouts in.

And less time exercising, meant less time with Amitie. This hurt more than she would like to admit…

It was about a week when others began to notice her condition. She was just outside school when another coughing fit came on. It sounded louder and harsher. She realized she wasn't just coughing, but coughing something up. Clutching her chest she finally got whatever it was out of her throat. She looked down expecting to be disgusted by her own phlem, but is instead confused at the sight of flower petals where she stood.

"Are you alright, Raffina?" She turned to see two of her classmates, Lidelle and Sig, coming behind her.

"I'm just fine, thank you. Why? Do I not look ok?" She replied, fluffing her hair, which had surely been ruffled in her fit.

"We heard you coming down," Sig atarted, "then you coughed a bunch of petals."

Raffina looked down at the petals again. Pink and yellow spots dotted the floor. They had come out of her? That made no sense. "Right, that. I suppose I caught some sort of cold."

"Where you cough flowers?"

"I don't know! It's probably… a spell gone wrong, or something. I'm sure it will wear off and I'll be good as new," Raffina brushed off the issue, not wanting to appear pitiful.

Sig seemed unconvinced and confused, but Lidelle had on a worried face, and she was staring down at the petals. "Petunias…" she whispered.

"Well, I should go get some stretching in before I run. Goodbye now," she said, before coughing again. This time it didn't last as long but it was still rough to get through. A couple more petals came out and it left her throat feeling sore.

"Maybe you should rest-oh," Lidelle started before Raffina ran off, not hearing her.

More time passed, and her condition didn't seem to get much better. It was more that Raffina got used to it, brushing it off as nothing to be worried about. She continued coughing whenever her chest pain increased. Flowers continued to come out. She could manage, she thought. These things take time.

It reached a head on a normal day of class. As usual their teacher spoke about puyo techniques. She was able to focus through her coughing, for the most part. She glanced over at Amitie, who was focused as much as she could be, trying hard to actually understand what was being said. Raffina sighed. That kind of determination in the face of adversity was quite admirable. Enviable, even. If she had that will, she could do anything…

And then it happened. Raffina began coughing again. But she couldn't stop. Her cough grew louder, and wetter, and soon it was all she could hear. Her breath was disappearing and she felt something slither out of her throat. By the time she had finally stopped, she was dizzy, and regaining her breath was hard. She looked around, seeing colored dots in her vision. Everyone else was looking at her. She groaned as she looked down at her desk, and on top was a full flower, stem and all, surrounded by wet petals and leaves. That was the last thing in her vision as she lay her head down and passed out.

Raffina didn't know how long it had been since she was unconscious. She woke up in the nurses office on a cot, laying on her side. The nurse had said she had cold like symptoms, but wasn't sure what exactly was wrong. So it wasn't anything she didn't know herself.

As she was about to leave the office, she heard voices just outside.

"It's odd isn't it? No one can figure out what the issue is and it's been like, forever now!" It was her friends talking about her.

"I think I know what's wrong, but it might be kind of strange…" Lidelle spoke up. "There's something called Hanahaki disease, where someone coughs up flowers. And the flowers pertain to something the patient is keeping to themself. In most of the cases I've heard of, they're in love but won't confess."

"So she's just in love with someone?"

"I don't know, it's just a common cause. But the longer you keep secrets like that, the more the flowers eat you up inside… you can get them removed surgically, but that means forgetting the source of your secrets. In the most common cases, the person you loved."

Raffina decided she heard enough. She exited the office just then, acting casually. For a while, nobody said anything, but the tension felt as think as her chest now did.

"Raffina, is there something you need to tell us?" Klug asked.

"No."

"Are you -"

"I'm quite sure. This… flower thing? I can handle it. I'll be fine."

"But how do you know? What if it keeps getting worse? You just have to say who you're in love with, then-" Amitie's mouth was covered by Sig.

"Don't be silly! There's no such thing. Like I said, it's likely some sort of error with magic, or something. I can figure it out, I'll be fine." Raffina looked around, and forced a smile. "I can handle this." She glanced again at Amitie before walking away, not letting anyone else speak.

She tried to handle it. She really did.

Raffina couldn't keep the flowers down around her family, and soon they began looking into treatments for the disease. There were plenty of suggested treatments (one even involving a weed whacker which they quietly ignored) but these only seemed to either delay or exacerbate the symptoms. They would eventually call it quits in this department, and urge Raffina to take the surgery to remove these flowers. Her friends tried to get her to admit to her feelings.

Raffina refused. She cited that such a surgery could go wrong and affect her workout schedule, and it wasn't worth that risk to her. And she refused to admit anything about her feelings too. That was for her to know and feel about. She couldn't live with that shame. She just couldn't.

She kept trying to look for hidden cures long after everyone else had stopped. She scoured countless books and folktales for anything, anything to make it stop, anything to keep it in. All the while she got weaker and weaker, the flowers taking up her energy and resolve. Eventually even she could no longer keep up the facade. She could barely get out of bed, much less her own home. She could hardly breathe, and her coughs produce several petunias a day, wet with her spit and blood.

It wasn't worth it to forget her. She just couldn't. She was a radiant beam and her life was darker without. But the hurt of rejection would be too much to bear too, she just knew it. This was her choice.

Raffina lay on her bed, surely her final resting place. It was littered with the stems and roots of blood stained petunias. The room was ridden with vibrant pinks and yellows connected with verdant greens and dots of red. It was almost a pretty sight, if one forgot the context. She laughed darkly, only for it to end in yet another flower on her bed.

She heard a knock by her door. It was ajar anyway, as she had become too sore to speak properly and yell to come in. The knock was simply a courtesy. Amitie pushed it open, looking around in shock at the view. "Raffina…" she trailed off, watching her friend slowly turn her head. She walked up closer to the bed.

"I know why you're here. I'll say it as much as I *cough, cough* have to. I won't do it. I won't." Raffina closed her eyes. She hoped that would be enough. But she knew Amitie all too well.

"Please, Raffina! Look at yourself! You can't even move anymore. What about all the working out? What about school and everyone? You're just gonna stop and give up!?"

"I'm just not like you, I guess." No, she wasn't like Amitie. Her boundless energy, naïve honesty, determination… she could never be like her. And that's why she had to keep her memory.

"What does that even mean? Ugh, why do you have to be so stubborn now, of all times!? Raffina, is this person even worth all this pain?"

Tears welled in Raffina's still closed eyes. She started moving. With the last of her strength, she stood up. Even as she coughed and choked, cmshe stood up and hugged her dear friend, as tight as she could manage.

"Yeah. She is."

Her lips touched Amitie's, every so slightly. And then she dropped, arms sliding down her back, to the ground. A last flower fell from her mouth, drenched in her blood, before she went limp.

Amitie kneeled in shock for a few seconds. In her mouth she tasted blood; a single petal fell out as she held it agape. She then realized what just happened. She tried to pick up Raffina, tried to lay her back on the bed, tried to get her to move, do anything, say anything. But there was nothing left. No sound, no movement. Just silence and flowers.

"Raffina… why didn't you tell me? You… I…" she cried on the bed, over the petals amd roots, over her friend's body.
